# Syllabus Forge style guide

Injected (first 3,000 characters) into every session by the SessionStart hook.

## Voice
- Second person, active voice, present tense. "You write the spec", not "The spec is written".
- Friendly and direct. No hype ("revolutionary", "game-changing"), no filler ("In today's world").
- One idea per paragraph, max 4 sentences. Headings say what the section teaches.

## Plain language
- Define every acronym and term on first use, then use the glossary term exactly.
- Prefer common words: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate".
- Avoid idioms and culture-specific references; many learners read in a second language.

## Inclusive language
- Gender-neutral by default ("they"). Varied, realistic names across cultures.
- No ability-based metaphors ("blind spot", "crippled"). No assumptions about age or background.

## Examples
- Fictional companies only: Northwind Labs (B2B analytics), Tidepool (consumer travel app).
- People: Priya (PM), Mateo (engineer), Ama (designer), Lukas (data lead).
- Emails use @example.com. No real customers, no real people's data.
- Model outputs in examples are labelled "Example output" and are illustrative.

## Citations
- Inline `[S#]` after the claim, matching `sources/registry.json`.
- Attribute recommendations: "Anthropic's prompting guide recommends..." [S4], not "You must...".
- Version-sensitive facts carry the date: "as of October 2026".

## Media
- Slides: max 20 words, min 24 px text, course theme only, highlight what the narration discusses.
- Diagrams: label directly on the figure; never rely on colour alone.
- The narrator is a synthetic voice; module 1 says so.

## AI disclosure
Every course credits: "Produced with AI assistance. Content reviewed and approved by <SME name>."
